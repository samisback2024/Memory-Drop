# Memory Drop

> **Capture today. Unlock tomorrow.**

Memory Drop is a social app built around delay instead of instant broadcast. Write a message, attach photos, video, audio, or a voice note, seal it with a future unlock date, and share it with the world — or keep it just for yourself. When the date arrives, it opens. Nothing about it is engagement-bait: no algorithmic feed, no ads, no ranking — you choose your tabs, and the app rewards patience instead of reflex.

**Live:** https://memory-drop-inky.vercel.app

## What Memory Drop is

**The problem it solves:** every other social app is built around *right now* — post it, everyone sees it in the next ten minutes, then it's buried by tomorrow's posts. Nothing rewards patience, and nothing gives a memory room to become more meaningful with time. Memory Drop is built around the opposite idea: some things are better *delayed* — a birthday message sealed for exactly the right morning, a "read this when you're older" note to your own future self, a whole year of moments that unlock on your anniversary.

**The core emotional loop:** anticipation → unlock → reflection. You don't come back for dopamine hits from other people's highlight reels — you come back because *something you're waiting for is about to open*, and everything you've ever unlocked lives forever in your own private archive, not an algorithm's timeline.

## The complete feature set

### Drops
The everyday unit of the app. A Drop is a photo, video, or short piece of text — share it instantly, or seal it with a future unlock date (a few hours from now, or years). You choose who can ever see it: Everyone, your Orbit, or Only Me. While it's sealed, nobody — including you — can peek at the real content; the server itself won't send it early. People who can see a sealed Drop can react to it while they wait (Interested / Can't Wait / Good Vibes / Save to Unlock), which is the app's alternative to a Like button for content that doesn't exist yet.

### Time Capsules
The signature feature and the app's namesake. A richer version of a Drop — combine photos, video, and voice recordings into one sealed package tied to a specific date, built through a guided 9-step wizard. This is the "open on our anniversary" / "for my kid's 18th birthday" use case — the thing people tell their friends about.

### Moments
The ephemeral counterpart. A quick photo, video, or text update visible for 12, 24, or 48 hours (your choice, every time) to Everyone, your Orbit, Close Friends, or Only Me. After it expires, it doesn't just vanish — it quietly lands in your own Memories archive forever. Nobody else can see it again, but you never lose it.

### Memories
Once something unlocks or expires, it becomes a permanent, searchable entry in your Memories library — browsable by Timeline, Calendar, Years, Collections, Favorites, Flashbacks, or Highlights. This is the archive nobody else curates for you: no algorithm decides what resurfaces, you do.

### Social graph — Orbit
Orbit / Orbit-request (for private accounts), block, mute, restrict, mutual-friends display, suggested friends, and full Orbiting-You/In-Orbit lists — all privacy-aware (a private account's content stays invisible to everyone but accepted Orbit members).

### Messaging
Real one-to-one chat: text, photos, video, voice notes, read receipts, typing indicators, reactions, replies, pinning, starring, and unsend/delete. **Messaging requires mutual Orbit** — you can only message someone once you're both in each other's Orbit, no exceptions, and no messages from strangers land in your inbox. You can also share someone's profile straight into a conversation — it renders as a live, tappable card, not just a link.

### Sparkle Drop, comments & reflections
"Like" is Sparkle Drop — a warm, on-brand reaction rather than a generic thumbs-up. Comments unlock once the content does; Reflect is a private, unlock-independent note-to-self available at any lock state, visible only to its author.

### Profile & avatars
A full profile with cover photo, bio, stats, and a genuinely original touch: a procedurally-generated avatar picker with **50 categories × 10 faces (500 unique options)** — nobody has to use a real photo if they'd rather not, and every face is generated by Memory Drop itself, not sourced from anywhere.

### Notifications
New orbiters, comments, reactions, messages, sign-in alerts — all real-time, all deduplicated correctly.

### Settings & privacy
Private account toggle, a toggle to hide reaction counts on your own locked content, dark mode, accessibility controls (font size, contrast, motion, touch targets), session management, and a **Deleted** tab — delete a Drop and it's recoverable for 30 days before it's gone for good, with a confirmation dialog in front of every destructive action in the app.

### Feels like a native app
Safe-area-aware layout for notched phones, directional page transitions (push/pop), a real drag-to-dismiss bottom sheet, edge-swipe-to-go-back, and pull-to-refresh throughout — built entirely with the platform's own touch/gesture APIs, no animation library added.

## Who it's for

Three sharpest wedges, don't try to be for everyone at once:

1. **Couples & close friend groups** — anniversary capsules, birthday surprises, inside-joke Moments. Highest emotional intensity, highest organic sharing rate.
2. **Parents** — "for my kid to open at 18," baby's-first-year capsules. Long time horizons, high retention (they *have* to come back), strong word-of-mouth in parenting communities.
3. **Journal-and-memory people** — the audience already using apps like Day One, Marco Polo, or private Instagram accounts as a personal archive. They already believe in the value of "future me will love this."

Pick **one** as the beachhead for the first 90 days of marketing (recommendation: #1 — couples/close friends — since a sealed capsule *requires* telling the other person to expect it, which is a fast, built-in viral loop).

## What it took to build this

Memory Drop was built entirely on Supabase — no custom backend server, no service-role key available to the build process. Every feature is a React component talking to Postgres through RLS-gated `SECURITY DEFINER` functions, and every piece of backend behavior shipped as a **numbered, additive SQL migration file** under `supabase/` — 60 files, `phase1` through `phase28`, never edited after the fact, always applied in order by hand in the Supabase SQL editor. That discipline is what let a single-developer, no-backend-team project ship 28 phases of real, interlocking features without ever having to reset the database.

A few architectural habits show up in almost every phase, on purpose:

- **Compute, don't duplicate.** Memories isn't its own table — it's Capsules, Moments, and Drops UNIONed at read time. Profile stats are computed live from the same tables Feed/Capsules/Memories already read, never a separately-tracked counter that could drift.
- **No peeking early, enforced by the server, not the UI.** Locked/sealed content is never sent to the client at all while it's sealed — not blurred, *absent* — so there's no way to inspect a network response and see your own future capsule early. This is true even for the capsule's own author.
- **Zero-new-dependency posture.** Gestures, drag-to-dismiss, pull-to-refresh, and page transitions are all hand-built on top of native touch events and CSS — not a reach for a library every time a native-feeling interaction was needed.
- **Manual QA over an automated suite.** There's no Jest/Vitest/Playwright test suite checked into the repo; every phase instead shipped a precise manual testing checklist. Later phases added a small Playwright driver script (kept outside the repo, in a scratch directory) for visual regression spot-checks during development.
- **Honesty over polish-by-omission.** Every phase's build notes called out what was deliberately *not* done and why, rather than letting a scope cut quietly look like a bug. That record is preserved in `git log` and in `KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md`, rather than as a 1,000-line changelog inside this file.

## Getting started

```bash
git clone https://github.com/samisback2024/Memory-Drop.git
cd Memory-Drop
npm install
npm run dev        # → http://localhost:5173
```

### Connecting Supabase

1. Create a project at [supabase.com](https://supabase.com)
2. Copy your **Project URL** and **anon public key** from Project Settings → API
3. Create a `.env` file at the project root:

```env
VITE_SUPABASE_URL=https://your-project.supabase.co
VITE_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY=your-anon-key
```

4. Run every file in `supabase/` **in order** via the Supabase SQL editor. The files are numbered (`phase1_auth.sql` → `phase28_mutual_orbit_messaging.sql`, 60 files total) and must be applied in that order — each one builds on the schema the last one left behind, and none of them are meant to be edited after the fact. `supabase/dev_seed_scale_test.sql` is **not a migration** — it's optional fixture data for load testing only, do not run it against a real/production project.

   Before assuming your project is fully up to date, check your Supabase SQL editor's migration history against the full file list in `supabase/` — a handful of the most recent phases (messaging behavior, admin-privilege lockdown, query performance) contain real behavior changes that aren't live until they're actually run.

5. Restart the dev server.

### Google OAuth setup

1. Google Cloud Console → create an OAuth client (Web application) with:
   - Authorized JavaScript origins: your app's URL(s) (e.g. `http://localhost:5173`, your Vercel domain)
   - Authorized redirect URI: `https://<your-project-ref>.supabase.co/auth/v1/callback`
2. Supabase dashboard → Authentication → Providers → Google → enable, paste the Client ID & Secret
3. Authentication → URL Configuration:
   - **Site URL:** your primary app URL
   - **Redirect URLs:** add every origin you use, each with `/**`, e.g. `http://localhost:5173/**` and `https://your-vercel-domain.vercel.app/**`

### Email sending

Supabase's built-in email sender has a very low rate limit (a handful of emails per hour) — fine for local testing, not enough for real signups. Before launch, configure a custom SMTP provider under **Authentication → Settings → SMTP Settings** (Resend, Postmark, and SendGrid all have free tiers that comfortably cover this).

## Security posture

Every table is protected by row-level security; almost nothing is trusted from the client beyond identity. A few deliberate, repeated architectural choices worth knowing about before extending this app:

- **Locked/sealed content is never sent to the client while it's sealed** — not hidden by the UI, genuinely absent from the RPC response.
- **Storage uses public-read buckets with unguessable, high-entropy paths**, not signed/expiring URLs — a deliberate tradeoff for free CDN caching over stronger revocation guarantees.
- **`moderation_status` and `is_admin` exist and are correctly gated**, but there is currently no admin UI to act on them — see [Known limitations](#current-state--read-before-you-launch-anything).
- **`profiles.is_admin` was previously self-grantable by any authenticated client** — found and closed in `phase26_admin_column_lockdown.sql`; make sure that migration is applied.

The full, section-by-section security review lives in `PRODUCTION_CHECKLIST.md`.

## What's next

### Phase 0 — Before you tell anyone

Ship the technical launch checklist first. A broken signup flow at 500 users kills word-of-mouth permanently:
- Run every pending migration (see [Current state](#current-state--read-before-you-launch-anything) above).
- Configure a real SMTP provider — Supabase's default sender caps out at a handful of emails per hour.
- Get a real Terms/Privacy legal pass — App Store/Play Store reviewers do check.
- Decide web-only vs. a native wrapper for launch. **Recommendation: launch web-first** — a PWA install is much lower-friction than an app-store review cycle. The full native-wrapper decision (Capacitor) and the complete phased submission plan for both stores live in `APP_STORE_SUBMISSION_PLAN.md`.

### Phase 1 — Seed community (target: first 1,000 real users)

Not a marketing phase — a product-market-fit phase. Don't buy ads yet.
1. Recruit 50–100 real people from your own network into the couples/close-friends wedge specifically. Ask each to seal one capsule for someone they care about, with a real future date.
2. Watch what actually gets people to come back — the unlock notification, the Moments FOMO, or the archive — and let that tell you which of the three audiences (see [Who it's for](#who-its-for)) to double down on.
3. Turn on `pg_cron` for `generate_unlock_reminders()` and `generate_weekly_recap()` — already-built engagement mechanics currently sitting unused in the codebase.

### Phase 2 — Public launch (target: 10,000–50,000 downloads)

Lean on the growth loops already built into the product:
- **Sealed capsules require telling someone.** "I just sent you something, it opens in 3 days" is an unpaid, organic invitation.
- **QR codes on unlocked content** — already built for sharing outside the app.
- **Unlock reminders** pull lapsed users back at exactly the moment they're most likely to re-engage.
- **"For my kid to open at 18"** is an inherently multi-decade retention hook no engagement-feed app can replicate.

Channels roughly in order of cost-effectiveness: content marketing around the emotional hook (this product's premise is more shareable than a feature list), community seeding in parenting/wedding/long-distance-relationship spaces, ASO once you're in the stores, micro-influencer seeding over celebrity spend, and a "the anti-Instagram" PR angle.

### Phase 3 — Scale (the path to a million)

Combine paid acquisition (only once you know real cohort retention math), a formal referral loop on top of the organic capsule-sharing loop, seasonal marketing pushes (Valentine's Day, graduation, New Year's), and the fact that every engagement mechanic already built (unlock reminders, weekly recap, the archive itself) lowers your effective cost per retained user over time.

### What to track before spending a dollar on acquisition

- **Activation** — % of signups who seal at least one Drop or Capsule in their first session.
- **D1/D7/D30 retention**, segmented by wedge audience.
- **Viral coefficient** — how many new signups each shared capsule/QR code/profile share generates.
- **Unlock-to-return rate** — of people notified their capsule/moment is ready, what % open the app within 24 hours.

The full, detailed version of this playbook — including the honest bottom line on what a million downloads actually requires — lives inline above; the app-store-specific execution plan is in `APP_STORE_SUBMISSION_PLAN.md`.
